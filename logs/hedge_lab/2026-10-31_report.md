All rules trip the same way (book down 8% → every risk sleeve halved, freed weight to SGOV; SGOV, USFR, KMLM exempt). They differ only in how the book gets its risk back. B1–B4 measure the next trip from the release point.

| Rule | Release (2 consecutive closes) |
|---|---|
| B0 | Current: book back above −5% and SPY above its 50-day |
| B1 | SPY above its 200-day |
| B2 | SPY and QQQ both above their 200-days |
| B3 | Tiered: growth sleeves (VGT, SMH, QQQ) return when QQQ is above its 200-day; every other risk sleeve when SPY is |
| B4 | B3, and each sleeve gets its weight back only when its own price is above its 200-day (until then that half waits in SGOV) |

## Known limits
- Convexity sleeve: option values are model marks (Black-Scholes, VIX as implied vol, the module's skew markup, 3% trading cost), on SPY's dividend-adjusted price. Real puts in a crash can fill worse or better; treat sleeve results as direction and size of effect, not a quote.
- KMLM runs on AQMIX from 2010 and T-bill cash before that, which understates the 2008 hedge in every variant equally.
- The entry gate is not simulated (in this regime it moves only SCHD +1, XLV +1, KMLM +3 vs base).
- The stress windows test today's book through past markets; they are not a replay of what the regime classifier would have chosen then (that needs full-history HY OAS, fix_status N11).
